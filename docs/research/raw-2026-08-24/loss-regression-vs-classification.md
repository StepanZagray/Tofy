# Literature review: losses for discrete grid next-state prediction

## TOPIC 1 — Regression vs classification for discrete/multimodal targets

**1. Stop Regressing: Training Value Functions via Classification for Scalable Deep RL** — Farebrother, Orbay, Vuong et al., 2024, ICML 2024, arXiv:2403.03950.
Core claim: replacing MSE/Huber regression on scalar values with categorical cross-entropy over a discretized support (HL-Gauss: Gaussian-smeared histogram targets, a soft generalization of two-hot) dramatically improves scaling and robustness. Quantitative: **+67% peak performance over regression** on scaled Atari; **1.8–2.1x** in multi-task Atari; **~30% better** when scaling with MoE; regression plateaus beyond ResNet-34 capacity while HL-Gauss keeps improving; gains attributed to cross-entropy's scale invariance, bounded/well-conditioned gradients, and implicit distributional representation learning. Implication for your model: even the *latent* Huber loss is on the wrong side of this result — the paper positions cross-entropy as a drop-in replacement for Huber wherever a network regresses continuous targets. (Precursor: Imani & White, "Improving Regression Performance with Distributional Losses," ICML 2018, arXiv:1806.04613.)

**2. DreamerV3** — Hafner et al., arXiv:2301.04104; journal version *Nature* 2025 ("Mastering diverse control tasks through world models"). Reward and critic heads use **symlog two-hot categorical cross-entropy** instead of regression, explicitly for robustness across target scales and multimodality; this is one of the tricks that lets one hyperparameter set work across 150+ tasks. Implication: two-hot/categorical heads are the proven default for world-model scalar heads.

**3. Start Classifying: Categorical Critics for LLM Reinforcement Learning** — Zhou et al., 2026, arXiv:2608.02181. HL-Gauss-style categorical critic (cross-entropy over discretized value support) consistently beats scalar-MSE PPO/DAPO critics on math-reasoning and search RL, with better Brier score/calibration and lower-variance advantages; ablations (one-hot vs two-hot vs Bernoulli) confirm the categorical formulation itself matters. A direct 2026 replication of the "stop regressing" effect in a new domain.

**4. Improving Deep Regression with Ordinal Entropy** — Zhang et al., ICLR 2023, arXiv:2301.08915. Analyzes *why* classification beats regression: cross-entropy learns **higher-entropy feature representations**, whereas MSE collapses feature diversity; also gradient-conditioning arguments (CE gradient norm bounded, MSE unbounded — see also the square-loss-vs-CE study arXiv:2006.07322 and XQC, arXiv:2509.25174, 2025, which ties deep-RL gains to better-conditioned optimization). Implication: switching your grounding head to CE should also improve the *latent representations* the JEPA learns, not just the decode.

**5. Mean-prediction pathology (the direct diagnosis of your 2–5% plateau).**
- **Diagnosing the conditional-mean barrier** — Chen, 2026, arXiv:2605.28076. Formalizes that squared-loss deterministic predictors converge to the conditional mean and cannot represent conditional variability; provides diagnostics separating underfitting from irreducible stochasticity. Huber's Bayes-optimal predictor is likewise a central-tendency statistic — for a multimodal discrete transition distribution the mean lies **between modes** and decodes to a wrong palette index.
- **Flow Matching in Feature Space for Stochastic World Modeling** — 2026, arXiv:2606.29059. States explicitly that deterministic predictors trained with pointwise regression "converge to the conditional mean or median, averaging over all plausible futures rather than representing any valid mode."
- Classic antecedent: **Deep multi-scale video prediction beyond mean square error** — Mathieu, Couprie, LeCun, ICLR 2016, arXiv:1511.05440 — L2 → blurry averaged frames; their fix (GDL + adversarial) consistently beat both L2 and **frame copying**.
- Key theoretical point for you: per-pixel softmax cross-entropy + **argmax decoding is MAP, not posterior-mean, decoding** — it returns the most probable color per pixel, which is exactly what "exact match" scores, while Huber-on-latents + nearest-palette decode returns a quantized conditional mean.

**6. Cross-entropy on tokens/pixels in world models.**
- **Genie** — Bruce et al., 2024, arXiv:2402.15391: action-conditioned MaskGIT dynamics transformer trained with **cross-entropy on discrete frame tokens** — the canonical modern grid-dynamics recipe.
- **Δ-IRIS / Efficient World Models with Context-Aware Tokenization** — Micheli et al., 2024, arXiv:2406.19320; and **masked-latent-transformer world models**, 2025, arXiv:2507.04075 — same pattern: discrete tokens + CE, no per-pixel regression anywhere.
- **ARC-AGI Without Pretraining (CompressARC)** — Liao et al., arXiv:2512.06104: a small network outputs **per-pixel color logits over the ARC palette trained with cross-entropy** — the closest published precedent to your exact output space; nobody in the ARC line regresses colors. (ARC-AGI-3 technical report: arXiv:2603.24621.)

## TOPIC 2 — Class imbalance, rare changed pixels, copy attractor

**7. Focal Loss** — Lin et al., ICCV 2017, arXiv:1708.02002. Down-weights easy (high-p) examples by (1−p)^γ; built precisely for extreme foreground/background imbalance (~1:1000), analogous to changed:unchanged pixels. Quantitative: RetinaNet + focal (γ=2, α=0.25) reached 39.1 AP, beating OHEM-based CE baselines by ~3+ AP.

**8. Revisiting Reweighted Risk for Calibration: AURC, Focal, and Inverse Focal Loss** — 2025, arXiv:2505.23463 (+ "A Note on the Stability of the Focal Loss," OpenReview). 2025 theory: focal loss is classification-calibrated (Bayes-optimal decision boundary preserved) but **not strictly proper** — probabilities are systematically underconfident; γ too high causes vanishing gradients, γ∈[1,3] empirically optimal. Implication: focal loss is safe for your argmax exact-match metric, but don't trust its probabilities, and tune γ low (1–2) first.

**9. Importance weighting: regime matters — and it favors you.**
- **What is the Effect of Importance Weighting in Deep Learning?** — Byrd & Lipton, ICML 2019, arXiv:1812.03372: for **overparameterized/separable** models the effect of loss reweighting **vanishes** as training proceeds.
- **Understanding the role of importance weighting for deep learning** — Xu et al., ICLR 2021 (implicit-bias formalization of the above).
- **Is Importance Weighting Incompatible with Interpolating Classifiers?** — Wang et al., ICLR 2022, arXiv:2112.12986: the vanishing effect comes from the **exponential tail** of CE; polynomially-tailed losses restore weighting's effect even at interpolation.
- Implication: your 560K model on a large transition dataset is firmly **underparameterized (non-interpolating)** — this is exactly the regime where per-pixel reweighting of CE *does* change the learned solution, so changed-pixel upweighting should have real, persistent effect (the Byrd–Lipton null result does not apply).
- **Long-Tail Learning via Logit Adjustment** — Menon et al., ICLR 2021, arXiv:2007.07314: additive log-prior logit offsets are **Fisher-consistent for balanced error**, with cleaner statistical grounding than naive weighting (also Cui et al. class-balanced loss, arXiv:1901.05555). Implication: for the 16-color marginal imbalance (background color dominates), logit adjustment by per-color pixel priors is a principled complement to change-mask weighting.

**10. Copy-map attractor / why rare changed pixels get starved.**
- **Gradient Starvation** — Pezeshki et al., NeurIPS 2021, arXiv:2011.09468: once an easy feature (here, the identity/copy map — ~95–99% of pixels) drives the loss down, gradients for harder features (change dynamics) are starved via coupled learning dynamics.
- **The Pitfalls of Simplicity Bias** — Shah et al., NeurIPS 2020, arXiv:2006.07710; **Simplicity Bias in 1-Hidden-Layer Networks** — Morwani et al., 2023, arXiv:2302.00457: nets provably latch onto the simplest predictive feature to the exclusion of others. The copy map is the maximally simple, ~95%-accurate-per-pixel shortcut; a uniform per-pixel loss makes it a strong local attractor — consistent with your plateau where unchanged pixels are right and changed pixels are ~random (2–5% exact-change match).
- **Copy baseline** in video prediction: PreCNet (arXiv:2004.14878) and the benchmark-review literature use copy-last-frame as the trivial baseline that naive L2 models hug; Mathieu et al. (arXiv:1511.05440) show loss redesign is what separates models from it.
- **Motion/change-weighted reconstruction**: motion-weighted patch losses that reweight reconstruction proportionally to **frame difference** are established in masked-video pretraining and future-frame anomaly work (e.g., motion-guided/MGMAE-style losses; saliency-weighted foreground loss, SIViP 2022); MOTIVE (ICML 2026 Oral, arXiv:2601.08828) shows motion-region weighting isolates the dynamics signal from static background. VaGraM/policy-shaped prediction (arXiv:2412.05766) is the RL analog: weight world-model loss by what matters. Implication: weighting loss by the change mask (frame-difference of the discrete grids — free to compute exactly in your setting) is the direct analog and is standard practice, just under the name "motion weighting."

## Ranked loss-design recommendations

1. **Replace the grounding loss with per-pixel 16-way softmax cross-entropy, decoded by argmax.** Strongest and most direct support: MAP decoding matches your exact-match metric, avoids the conditional-mean barrier (arXiv:2605.28076, 2606.29059, 1511.05440), matches every modern discrete world model (Genie 2402.15391, Δ-IRIS 2406.19320) and the ARC precedent (2512.06104), and inherits CE's conditioning/representation benefits (2403.03950, 2301.08915).
2. **Add changed-pixel reweighting to that CE** (e.g., weight w≫1 on pixels where target ≠ previous frame, computed exactly from the discrete grids). Supported by motion-weighted loss practice and, critically, by the importance-weighting theory: your underparameterized 560K model is in the regime where reweighting provably shifts the solution (1812.03372 / 2112.12986 read jointly). This directly attacks the copy attractor / gradient starvation (2011.09468, 2006.07710).
3. **Focal loss (γ≈1–2) as an adaptive alternative or complement to fixed change weights** — self-anneals as the copy map is mastered, proven under 1:1000 imbalance (1708.02002); keep γ modest and don't rely on its probabilities (2505.23463).
4. **Logit adjustment / class-prior offsets over the 16 colors** for the rare-color marginal imbalance — Fisher-consistent, cheaper to tune than per-class weights (2007.07314).
5. **Replace the latent Huber with HL-Gauss/two-hot cross-entropy on discretized latent targets** (or at least symlog) — the "stop regressing" result applies to the latent head too and is the best-evidenced accuracy lever after items 1–2 (2403.03950: +67%, 1.8–2.1x; 2301.04104; 2608.02181).
6. **Optional structural fix if the plateau persists:** factor prediction into a change-mask head + color head (predict *where* change happens, then *what* color), the discrete analog of motion-selective prediction (arXiv:1812.10157) and mask-based world models (2604.19683) — turns the imbalance into an explicit, controllable binary problem.
