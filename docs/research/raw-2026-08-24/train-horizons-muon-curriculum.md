# TOPIC 5 — Open-loop horizon curricula and compounding error

**1. Learning with Imperfect Models: When Multi-step Prediction Mitigates Compounding Error** — Somalwar, Lee, Pappas, Matni, 2025, [arXiv:2504.01766](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01766)
Core claim (linear systems, rigorous): when the model class is **well-specified**, single-step training achieves *lower* asymptotic prediction error than multi-step training; multi-step direct prediction only wins when the class is **misspecified** (e.g., partial observability), where it reduces bias. They also evaluate exactly your setup — "a single-step model trained with a multi-step loss" — as an intermediate strategy.
Implication: this is the most direct theoretical answer to "does long-horizon training degrade one-step accuracy?" — **yes, if your model class can represent the true one-step dynamics**. For a 560K model on synthetic grid tasks where the one-step rule is deterministic and fully observed, multi-step Huber loss with horizon up to max is predicted to *raise* one-step error by trading variance/exactness for long-horizon bias reduction you may not need. If your metric is one-step changed-pixel exactness, the horizon curriculum is likely working against the metric.

**2. Is Behavior Cloning All You Need? Understanding Horizon in Imitation Learning** — Foster, Block, Misra, NeurIPS 2024, [arXiv:2407.15007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.15007)
Core claim: the classic Ross & Bagnell O(T²) vs O(T) gap (behavior cloning vs DAgger, [arXiv:1011.0686](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0686)) is not fundamental: with **log-loss and a realizable function class**, offline BC achieves horizon dependence H·log|Π|/n (deterministic experts) — no quadratic compounding. Compounding error is a consequence of loss choice/misspecification, not of open-loop prediction per se.
Implication: with an exact-match-style objective on deterministic dynamics, good one-step models need not compound quadratically. Fixing one-step exactness (currently 2–5%) matters far more than horizon training; horizon curricula are treating the symptom.

**3. The Pitfalls of Imitation Learning when Actions are Continuous** — Simchowitz et al., 2025, [arXiv:2503.09722](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.09722)
Core claim: in continuous-action settings, **exponential compounding error is unavoidable** by any learning-procedure modification alone; complementary work shows action chunking / trajectory-level prediction gives exponential improvements ([arXiv:2507.09061](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09061)).
Implication: your latent-JEPA rollouts live in a continuous embedding space; if long-horizon fidelity is ever needed, structural fixes (chunked/multi-step heads, discretization) beat loss-schedule fixes. But for a one-step discrete-pixel metric this is secondary.

**4. Huszár — How (not) to Train your Generative Model: Scheduled Sampling, Likelihood, Adversary?** — 2015, [arXiv:1511.05101](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.05101)
Core claim: scheduled sampling's implicit objective is **improper** — even with infinite data it does not converge to the data distribution and pushes models to ignore conditioning context.
Implication: your horizon curriculum (2→4→8→max) is a cousin of scheduled sampling (mixing teacher-forced and free-running gradients). Huszár's inconsistency result warns that late-stage long-horizon training can degrade the conditional (one-step) model itself, consistent with a one-step accuracy plateau.

**5. Rollout-error analyses in learned simulators (2024–26)** — [Understanding Rollout Error in Graph World Models, arXiv:2606.27780](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27780v2); [Repair the Amplifier, Not the Symptom, arXiv:2607.01767](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.01767v2); [A Definition and Roadmap for World Models, arXiv:2607.06401](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06401v1); [Recurrent Neural Operators, arXiv:2505.20721](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.20721); [PDE-Refiner, arXiv:2308.05732](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/d529b943af3dba734f8a7d49efcb6d09-Paper-Conference.pdf)
Core claims: rollout error grows superlinearly via Lipschitz amplification of one-step error; unrolled training improves *temporal stability* of long rollouts, and the PDE/weather-surrogate literature consistently frames this as a **trade**: unrolled/multi-step training buys stability at long lead times, while the sharpest one-step models are trained one-step (with noise injection — the "pushforward trick," [arXiv:2202.03376](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03376) — as a cheaper stability substitute that does *not* sacrifice one-step accuracy).
Implication: if the eval metric is one-step exactness, the literature's cheap alternative is **one-step loss + input noise/perturbation** rather than open-loop unrolling; keep at most a small-weight auxiliary rollout loss.

**Topic 5 verdict:** evidence points both ways for long-horizon *stability*, but for *one-step exactness* on a well-specified deterministic system, the 2025 theory (2504.01766, 2407.15007) says the horizon curriculum is likely diluting gradient signal for the metric you actually report.

# TOPIC 6 — Muon theory and batch-size / schedule theory

**Muon lineage and interpretation**
- **Muon** — Keller Jordan et al., 2024 (blog "Muon: An optimizer for hidden layers in neural networks"; no arXiv id) — Newton–Schulz orthogonalized Nesterov momentum; empirically strong on small nanoGPT-scale models.
- **Old Optimizer, New Norm: An Anthology** — Bernstein & Newhouse, 2024, [arXiv:2409.20325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20325) (and Modular Duality, arXiv:2410.21265). Core claim: Muon-style updates = **steepest descent under the spectral norm**; each layer should get an operator norm matched to its role. Implication: your final/embedding-like layers (and any 1D params) should stay on Adam — check that only genuine hidden matrices go through the Muon path; norm-mismatched layers are a known small-model failure source.
- **Muon is Scalable for LLM Training** — Liu, Su et al. (Moonshot/Kimi), 2025, [arXiv:2502.16982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16982). Core claims: Muon needs (1) **decoupled weight decay** and (2) a per-parameter **0.2·√max(fan_in,fan_out) RMS-matching rescale** so updates match AdamW's RMS, letting you reuse AdamW lr/wd; ~2× compute efficiency vs AdamW at compute-optimal scale. Implication: verify your DeepSeek-style normalized-buffer variant applies the shape-dependent rescale; with lr 1e-3 and wd 0.01 shared across Muon and Adam groups, the update-RMS matching is what makes that sharing valid. Without it, effective lr differs per layer shape — a real risk at odd small-model shapes.
- **Convergence notes/theory**: [A Note on the Convergence of Muon, arXiv:2502.02900](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02900) (Li & Hong); Pethick et al. LMO-norm-ball analysis; [On the Convergence of Muon and Beyond, arXiv:2509.15816](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15816v5); a 2026 negative result [Muon Does Not Converge on Convex Lipschitz Functions, arXiv:2605.08980](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.08980) (non-smooth caveat only). Also [Muon Outperforms Adam in Tail-End Associative Memory Learning, arXiv:2509.26030](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.26030) — Muon's advantage concentrates on **heavy-tail/rare features**, relevant since rare pixel-transition rules are exactly your failure mode.
- **Convergence Bound and Critical Batch Size of Muon** — Sato, Naganuma, Iiduka, 2025, [arXiv:2507.01598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01598). Core claim: convergence bounds with/without Nesterov and weight decay; weight decay yields almost-sure boundedness of parameter/gradient norms; derives a **lower bound on Muon's critical batch size** governed by momentum β and weight decay λ (qualitative — absolute CBS not predictable from the formula). Implication: your momentum 0.95 + wd 0.01 setting is in the theoretically covered regime; CBS grows with momentum, partially legitimizing larger batches under Muon.
- **Practical Efficiency of Muon for Pretraining** — Essential AI, 2025, [arXiv:2505.02222](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.02222). Core claim: Muon **retains data efficiency at large batch sizes** much better than AdamW, expanding the compute-time Pareto frontier. Implication: Muon is the right choice *given* batch 2048, but doesn't repeal CBS entirely.
- **Fantastic Pretraining Optimizers and Where to Find Them** — Wen et al., 2025, [arXiv:2509.02046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02046) (+ sequel [arXiv:2606.16899](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16899)). Core claim: matrix optimizers' speedup is **largest at small scale** (~1.4× over AdamW at 0.1B, shrinking to 1.1× at 1.2B), and many claimed gains vanish under fair tuning; sequel attributes shrinkage to constant weight decay and fixes it with "hyperball" (spectral-norm-constrained) training. Implication: at 560K params Muon is well-supported; but re-tune lr *jointly* with wd — mis-tuned baselines are the #1 confound this paper documents.
- **muP/lr-transfer for Muon**: [Spectral Condition for μP under Width–Depth Scaling, arXiv:2603.00541](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00541), [Towards a Principled Muon under µP, arXiv:2601.01306](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.01306), [Optimal Scaling Needs Optimal Norm, arXiv:2510.03871](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03871v1), and [Weight Decay may matter more than μP for LR Transfer in Practice, arXiv:2510.19093](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19093). Core claims: spectral-condition scaling gives stable lr transfer for Muon+AdamW hybrids; notably, **weight decay choice can dominate μP** for practical lr transfer. Implication: your lr=1e-3/wd=0.01 pair tuned once will not automatically transfer if you scale the model; use the spectral condition + update-RMS matching rather than re-sweeping.

**Critical batch size**
- **How Does Critical Batch Size Scale in Pre-training?** — Zhang, Morwani, Vyas et al., ICLR 2025, [arXiv:2410.21676](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21676). Core claim: **CBS scales primarily with data size, not model size** (theory via infinite-width limits; experiments 85M–1.2B).
- **Critical Batch Size Revisited** — Merrill, Arora, Groeneveld, Hajishirzi, 2025, [arXiv:2505.23971](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23971). Core claims: CBS is **≈0 at initialization, grows, then plateaus**; gradient-noise-scale (McCandlish et al. 2018, arXiv:1812.06162) **underestimates CBS by orders of magnitude** and is unreliable; **batch-size warmup** matched OLMo-1B loss with 43% fewer steps.
- [Adaptive Batch Sizes Using Non-Euclidean Gradient Noise Scales, arXiv:2602.03001](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.03001) extends noise-scale reasoning to spectral-descent (Muon-geometry) optimizers.
Is 2048 beyond CBS for 560K params? Model size per se isn't the determinant (2410.21676), but **data-per-run and training phase are**: with only ~28K steps, batch 2048 is almost certainly above CBS for the early phase of every lesson (CBS ≈ 0 at init — and your sequential curriculum effectively "re-initializes" the data distribution 5 times). Expect degraded per-sample efficiency early in each lesson; batch-size warmup (e.g., 128→2048 within each lesson/run) is the literature's concrete fix.

**Schedules**
- **Understanding WSD LRs: A River Valley Loss Landscape Perspective** — Wen et al., ICLR 2025, [arXiv:2410.05192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05192). Core claim: loss is a river valley; the stable high-lr phase makes progress "along the river" while oscillating; the **decay phase harvests it** with a sharp loss drop. Follow-ups: [Multi-Power Law for schedule loss prediction, arXiv:2503.12811](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.12811); [cooldown-stage dynamics, arXiv:2508.01483](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.01483).
- **The Road Less Scheduled** — Defazio, Yang, Mehta, Mishchenko, Khaled, Cutkosky, 2024, [arXiv:2405.15682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15682). Schedule-free averaging matches/exceeds cosine schedules with no horizon commitment; won AlgoPerf 2024 self-tuning track.
Implication: if you're evaluating checkpoints mid-run at constant lr 1e-3, you are systematically **underestimating the model** — a proper decay branch (WSD) before each eval can move accuracy materially; alternatively evaluate the schedule-free averaged iterate. Your 2–5% plateau may partly be an un-decayed-checkpoint artifact.

# TOPIC 7 — Curriculum learning and forgetting

- **Simple and Scalable Strategies to Continually Pre-train LLMs** — Ibrahim et al., 2024, [arXiv:2403.08763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08763). Core claim: lr re-warm + re-decay + **replay** matches full retraining. Quantitative: **5% replay suffices for weak distribution shift; 25% for strong shift** (25% replay cut forgetting by 1.23 nats of final loss at 405M scale). Implication: your 5 synthetic lessons are *strong* shifts; sequential staging with 0% replay predicts severe forgetting of lessons 1–4 by the end — mix **~25% replay of all prior lessons** into each stage, and re-warm/re-decay lr per lesson.
- **Revisiting Replay and Gradient Alignment for Continual Pre-Training** — 2025, [arXiv:2508.01908](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.01908): replay remains the strongest simple baseline; gradient-alignment adds marginally.
- **Scalable Strategies for Continual Learning with Replay** — 2025, [arXiv:2505.12512](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12512v1): add scaled replay loss to every batch rather than block-scheduling it.
- **When Do Curricula Work?** — Wu, Dyer, Neyshabur, ICLR 2021, [arXiv:2012.03107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03107) (still the reference negative result): on standard benchmarks with i.i.d. access, **curricula and anti-curricula perform no better than random order**; ordering helps mainly under limited budget or noisy data. **An Analytical Theory of Curriculum Learning in Teacher-Student Networks** ([arXiv:2106.08068](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.08068)) shows curriculum gains are modest and easily erased in the long-training limit.
- **What Makes a Good Curriculum?** — 2025, [arXiv:2510.19099](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.19099) and **Curriculum Learning for LLM Pretraining: An Analysis of Learning Dynamics** — 2026, [arXiv:2601.21698](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.21698v2): ordering effects exist but are dominated by model-side difficulty signals; benefits concentrate in data-constrained regimes; **mixing with a drifting mixture ("staged mixing") beats hard staging** in modern pretraining practice (see also [Data Mixing survey, arXiv:2604.16380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16380), [curriculum via ordering, arXiv:2506.11300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11300)).
Implication: the literature favors **annealed mixture proportions over hard sequential lessons**. Hard staging + no replay is the single most theory-contradicted element of the described setup, and forgetting of early-lesson primitives plausibly explains poor held-out transfer.

# Ranked recommendations

1. **Add replay across lessons or switch to annealed mixing.** Replace the hard 5-lesson sequence with a mixture whose proportions drift toward later lessons, or keep stages but include ~25% replay of all earlier lessons in every batch (strong-shift figure from arXiv:2403.08763; mechanism support: 2508.01908, 2505.12512). Highest expected impact on held-out one-step accuracy.
2. **Decay the learning rate before every evaluation.** Constant-lr checkpoints understate the model (river-valley result, arXiv:2410.05192). Use WSD with a short decay branch per eval, or schedule-free averaging (arXiv:2405.15682). Cheap and may immediately move the 2–5% number.
3. **Re-weight the horizon curriculum toward one-step.** Theory says multi-step loss trades away one-step exactness when the model class is well-specified (arXiv:2504.01766); scheduled-sampling-style mixing is improper (arXiv:1511.05101). Keep a dominant one-step term (optionally with input-noise/pushforward for stability, arXiv:2202.03376) and make horizons 4/8/max a small auxiliary weight — or ablate the curriculum entirely against pure one-step training.
4. **Adopt batch-size warmup instead of flat 2048.** CBS is near zero early and grows with data seen, not model size (arXiv:2505.23971, 2410.21676); with 28K steps and repeated distribution shifts, flat 2048 wastes samples early in each lesson. Warm 128→2048; Muon mitigates but does not remove the penalty (arXiv:2505.02222, 2507.01598).
5. **Audit the Muon implementation for update-RMS matching and parameter routing.** Ensure the 0.2·√max(fan_in,fan_out)-style rescale (arXiv:2502.16982) so lr 1e-3/wd 0.01 shared with Adam is coherent per layer shape, and that embeddings/output/1D params are on Adam per operator-norm assignment (arXiv:2409.20325). Then do one joint lr×wd sweep — mis-tuned wd is the documented cause of vanishing Muon gains (arXiv:2509.02046, 2606.16899).
6. **If long-horizon fidelity is a real goal later, use structural fixes, not longer unrolls:** multi-step/chunked prediction heads (exponential-gap results, arXiv:2503.09722, 2507.09061) or refinement-style rollout correction (PDE-Refiner arXiv:2308.05732), rather than pushing the open-loop horizon curriculum harder.
7. **Treat curriculum ordering itself with skepticism.** Given arXiv:2012.03107 and 2106.08068, run a random-mixture control; if it matches the curriculum, the lesson sequencing machinery is complexity you can delete.
