# Research report: raising changed-pixel EXACT accuracy for Tofy

## Headline conclusion

The 2024–2026 literature is close to unanimous: for exact-match prediction over discrete state spaces, **per-token/per-pixel categorical cross-entropy decoding beats latent regression**, and the single best-supported add-on for near-deterministic grids is an explicit **copy-vs-generate (change-sparsity) mechanism**. Your current setup (Huber latent regression + frozen EXACT decoder, LeWM lineage — LeWorldModel itself is arXiv 2603.19312, 2026) optimizes a proxy that is not aligned with exact match; every token-based world model that reports strong pixel-exact rollouts trains CE directly on discrete targets.

## Direction 1: Discrete/categorical decoding

**1. Per-pixel factored 16-way CE head (top priority).** Add a trainable decoder head (1×1 conv, 128→16 logits per cell) on the predicted latent, trained with per-pixel cross-entropy against the true next grid — as an auxiliary to (or replacement for) the Huber latent loss. Why it helps: CE gives calibrated argmax-able distributions and much better-conditioned gradients than regression; "Stop Regressing" (Farebrother et al., arXiv 2403.03950, ICML 2024) shows categorical CE beats MSE regression across RL domains even for inherently continuous targets; all exact-token world models (IRIS 2209.00588, TWM 2303.07109, DART 2406.01361, Delta-IRIS 2406.19320, ITC below) use CE. Your 16-color grid needs no VQ tokenizer — pixels already are tokens, which removes the usual tokenizer-error floor. Candle cost: trivial (conv + `cross_entropy`), zero inference cost change. Risk: gradient interference with the EP/JEPA objective (tune loss weight); with 98%+ unchanged pixels plain CE is dominated by "copy" — must reweight (see idea 2). Established.

**2. Copy-vs-generate factorization / changed-mask head.** Predict per pixel: (a) a binary "changed" gate and (b) color logits; final output = gate·argmax(color) + (1−gate)·previous pixel, with the CE loss upweighted or restricted on changed pixels (focal-style). This directly optimizes your metric and fixes the class imbalance that plateaus you at 2–5%. Strongest recent evidence: **ITC — Identifiable Token Correspondence** (arXiv 2605.16457, 2026) assigns each next-frame token to "copy from previous frame" or "generate," via a Sinkhorn OT solver at decode time, is a post-hoc single-pass step adding ~2.8% compute, and set the May-2026 Atari-100k SOTA (IQM 1.092) precisely by exploiting frame persistence in grid-like environments. Delta-IRIS (arXiv 2406.19320, ICML 2024) is the same insight at the tokenizer level (encode only inter-frame deltas), 10× faster than IRIS, SOTA Crafter. Candle cost: one extra sigmoid head + BCE; the simple gated variant needs no OT solver (the Sinkhorn version is also implementable — matmuls + exp — but likely unnecessary at 64×64 with fixed positions, since your grid has no token-position ambiguity). Risk: gate must not learn "never change"; balance BCE with pos-weighting. Gated-copy per-palette-pixel is a light novel combination of established pieces; ITC/Delta-IRIS themselves are established.

**3. MaskGIT-style iterative refinement (optional, K small).** EMERALD (arXiv 2507.04075, 2025) shows MaskGIT-style masked-latent prediction over spatial latents yields more accurate world-model trajectories than compressed-vector Dreamer latents (first to beat human experts on Crafter ≤10M steps); REM/POP (arXiv 2402.05643, 2024) and TECO-lineage work ("Masked Generative Priors Improve World Models," arXiv 2410.07836) agree. Compatible with your constraint if you allow K=2–4 forward passes: predict all 4096 cells in parallel, re-mask low-confidence cells, re-predict. Candle cost: moderate — needs a (local or factorized) attention block over the 64×64 grid conditioned on previous state; at 560K params use your existing conv trunk plus one small attention layer. Risk: at 9s/step, K passes multiply eval cost; independent parallel sampling needs ≥2 steps to capture inter-cell dependencies; benefit is smallest in fully deterministic games where a single confident pass should suffice — try only if idea 1+2 plateau.

**4. DreamerV3 tricks — selective transfer** (Hafner et al., arXiv 2301.04104, 2023/Nature 2025). Transfers: **unimix** (mix 1% uniform into categorical outputs — cheap, prevents zero-probability logits and stabilizes CE), **free bits** on any regularizer, and **categorical straight-through latents** if you ever want a discrete bottleneck state. Does NOT transfer: symlog and two-hot (designed for unbounded continuous rewards/values — your targets are already 16-way discrete), and KL balancing (needs a stochastic prior you don't have). Established; the deterministic-grid restriction is my analysis, not published ablation. No paper directly ablates pixel-CE vs JEPA-latent objectives in deterministic environments; closest evidence is negative-for-reconstruction only on *control* performance (LeWM lineage), not on exact-match prediction — for exact match the token-model literature implies CE wins. This gap is real: adding a pixel-CE grounding head to a LeJEPA/EP model is an unpublished combination.

## Direction 2: Transformer world models / latent actions

**5. Delta-JEPA action decoder as auxiliary loss** (arXiv 2606.31232, 2026). Add a small head that predicts the executed action from the latent difference ẑ_{t+1}−z_t. Two-loss recipe (latent prediction + action reconstruction) markedly improved action-sensitivity and planning success (100% on Two-Room). Why it helps you: at 560K params your ACTION6 (x,y) conditioning is the likeliest failure point; this loss forces transitions to be action-discriminable, which is exactly what changed-pixel prediction requires. Candle cost: tiny MLP + CE over 6 action types (+ regression/CE over coordinates). Risk: in mostly-static frames Δz≈0 for no-op transitions — mask those. Established method, new domain.

**6. ARC-AGI-3-specific context.** The only published ARC-AGI-3 world-model results use **executable (code) world models**, not neural ones ("Executable World Models for ARC-AGI-3," arXiv 2605.05138, 2026: GPT-5.5 agent, 15/25 games; no next-state accuracy metric reported), plus graph-based exploration (arXiv 2512.24156). No published neural next-state-prediction baseline exists for ARC-AGI-3 — your 2–5% number has no literature comparison, and a working discrete-decoding model would be a novel result. "ARC Is a Vision Problem!" (arXiv 2511.14761, 2025) confirms the framing that single-pixel errors void exact match, motivating changed-pixel-weighted CE over uniform losses.

**7. Token-transformer dynamics (lower priority at your scale).** IRIS/TWM/STORM/DART all pair VQ tokens with an autoregressive transformer; at 560K params and 64×64=4096 cells, full AR decoding violates your cheap-forward-pass constraint and the VQ tokenizer is redundant given the native palette. Take their *design elements* (CE decoding, spatial tokens, copy priors — ideas 1–2) rather than the architecture. "Probing the Impact of Scale on Data-Efficient Generalist Transformer World Models for Atari" (arXiv 2605.08578, 2026) suggests these architectures reward scale you don't have.

## Priority order

1. Per-pixel 16-way CE head with changed-pixel upweighting (ideas 1+2 combined; days of work, directly optimizes the metric).
2. Copy-gate head (part of the same change).
3. Delta-JEPA action-reconstruction auxiliary (one small head).
4. Unimix + free bits (one-line stabilizers).
5. MaskGIT K-step refinement (only if 1–3 plateau).
6. ITC-style OT decoding (only if object *motion* — shifted tokens — turns out to be the dominant error mode).
