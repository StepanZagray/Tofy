Literature review complete. Findings below, organized by the five requested topics, with a synthesis for Tofy at the end.

## 1. Sokoban with learned models: learned search vs explicit search

**Thinker: Learning to Plan and Act** — arXiv [2307.14993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14993), NeurIPS 2023 (Chung et al., Cambridge/DeepMind lineage).
- Domain: Sokoban (Boxoban), Atari.
- Planning type: **explicit-in-latent** — the agent gets meta-actions to roll out its *learned* world model and inspect imagined futures before committing to a real action; the search policy itself is learned, not handcrafted MCTS.
- Evidence: 94.5% Sokoban solve rate at 5e7 frames vs 56.7% for the identical RL algorithm on the raw MDP — a direct, controlled measurement that querying a learned model at decision time adds ~38 points on a puzzle domain.
- Tofy relevance: **highest-relevance architecture pattern in this sweep.** Same shape as Tofy's setting (action-conditioned learned model, deterministic puzzle, decision-time search). Caveat: Thinker's world model was trained jointly with dense environment reward; Tofy has hidden goals, so the "which imagined future is good" signal must come from elsewhere (novelty/empowerment/learned progress).

**Planning in a recurrent neural network that plays Sokoban** — arXiv [2407.15421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.15421) (Taufeeque et al., Jul 2024, ICML MechInterp workshop) and follow-up **Path Channels and Plan Extension Kernels: a Mechanistic Description of Planning in a Sokoban RNN** — arXiv [2506.10138](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.10138) (Jun 2025).
- Domain: Sokoban, DRC (ConvLSTM) agent from Guez et al.'s "model-free planning" line.
- Planning type: **implicit-in-weights** (emerges inside a generic recurrent net; no explicit model or search code).
- Evidence: plans (future box-push routes) are linearly decodable from ConvLSTM channels; giving extra "thinking steps" at test time causally improves solves on hard levels; the mechanism is spatial "path channels" extended step-by-step by learned kernels; generalizes to larger unseen puzzles. Planning emerges within ~70M steps.
- Tofy relevance: strong existence proof that a **tiny ConvLSTM (~1M params) develops internal search on 2D grids** given enough deterministic-puzzle RL experience — supports the bet that a small recurrent predictor over Tofy-scale grids can amortize search, but note it took reward supervision and tens of millions of env steps.

**Interpreting Emergent Planning in Model-Free Reinforcement Learning** — arXiv [2504.01871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01871), **ICLR 2025 oral** (Bush et al.).
- Same DRC/Sokoban setting; planning type implicit-in-weights.
- Evidence: concept probes + causal interventions show the agent internally formulates plans predicting long-term action effects; emergence of plans coincides with the ability to benefit from extra test-time compute.
- Tofy relevance: confirms 2407.15421 with stronger causal methodology; together these are the canonical "emergent planning in small nets on grids" citations.

**Searchformer (Beyond A*)** — arXiv [2402.14083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14083) (Meta, Feb 2024).
- Domain: mazes, Sokoban.
- Planning type: **explicit-in-symbolic, executed in tokens** — a transformer trained to emit A*'s search trace, then bootstrapped to shorten it.
- Evidence: solves 93.7% of unseen Sokoban puzzles optimally with up to 26.8% fewer search steps than the teacher A*; search-trace training beats solution-only training with 5–10× smaller models/data.
- Tofy relevance: moderate. Requires a symbolic solver to generate traces (Tofy has none — rules are hidden), but the meta-lesson transfers: *training on process, not just outcomes, is far more sample-efficient for small models*.

**Dualformer** — arXiv [2410.09918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09918) (Meta, ICLR 2025).
- Maze + Sokoban; explicit-in-symbolic (token traces) with learned trace-dropping.
- Evidence: 97.6% on unseen 30×30 mazes vs Searchformer's 93.3% with 45.5% fewer reasoning steps; auto mode 96.6% with 59.9% fewer steps.
- Tofy relevance: low directly (same teacher-solver dependency), but shows learned search can be *compressed* below the explicit search's cost — the strongest published "learned search beats the explicit search it imitated" result on grid puzzles.

**Stream of Search** — arXiv [2404.03683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03683) (Gandhi et al., 2024). Countdown arithmetic, not grids; explicit search serialized into language; SoS pretraining +25% accuracy over optimal-trajectory-only training, and STaR/APA fine-tuning solves 36% of problems unsolved by the teacher heuristics. Relevance: reinforces the Searchformer lesson only; skip for Tofy's grid setting.

**DreamerV3 on Sokoban:** I found **no credible published DreamerV3 Sokoban solve-rate result** (checked the Nature 2025 version, GitHub, and follow-ups like MuDreamer). Sokoban model-based baselines in the literature are MuZero and Thinker (Thinker paper reports MuZero underperforming its variant at matched frames). Treat "DreamerV3 does X on Sokoban" claims as unsupported.

## 2. Discrete/token world models on grid games — used for planning or only dreaming?

**Δ-IRIS (Efficient World Models with Context-Aware Tokenization)** — arXiv [2406.19320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19320), ICML 2024 (Micheli et al.).
- Crafter; discrete delta-tokens + autoregressive transformer.
- **Dreaming only** — the model trains a policy in imagination; no decision-time search. SOTA Crafter (17/22 tasks at 10M frames), beats DreamerV3 past 3M frames, 10× faster training than IRIS.

**DIAMOND** — arXiv [2405.12399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12399), NeurIPS 2024 spotlight. Diffusion world model, Atari 100k HNS 1.46. **Dreaming only.** Also too slow per imagined step for Tofy's 0.1–2 s action budget if used for deep search.

- Pattern across IRIS/Δ-IRIS/DIAMOND/STORM-class token world models: **none of the flagship results use the model for decision-time planning on grid games** — they use it as a data amplifier for a reactive policy. The exceptions that do plan at decision time are the MuZero lineage: **UniZero** (arXiv [2406.10667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10667), transformer latent world model + MCTS) and 2026 object-centric variants (OC-STORM+MCTS, arXiv [2601.06604](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2601.06604); COMET, arXiv [2606.14418](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14418)) — but these are evaluated on Atari/robotics/VizDoom, not Sokoban-class puzzles.
- Tofy implication: decision-time latent search on grid puzzles is comparatively **underexplored territory**; the published wins there are Thinker and DeepCubeAI (below), not the token-world-model mainstream.

## 3. ARC specifically

**HRM (Hierarchical Reasoning Model)** — arXiv [2506.21734](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21734) (Sapient, Jun 2025, 27M params) and the **ARC Prize independent analysis** ([blog](https://arcprize.org/blog/hrm-analysis), [repo](https://github.com/arcprize/hierarchical-reasoning-model-analysis)).
- Planning type: **implicit latent iteration** (recurrent refinement in latent space).
- Evidence, corrected by ARC Prize on hidden tasks: **32% ARC-AGI-1, 2% ARC-AGI-2** (below the paper's claims). Ablations: the hierarchy is nearly irrelevant — a similarly sized vanilla transformer matches it; the gains come from **outer-loop iterative refinement** and per-task augmentation (~300 augs suffice); performance is largely transductive/task-specific rather than transfer.
- **TRM (Less is More: Recursive Reasoning with Tiny Networks)** — arXiv [2510.04871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04871) (Jolicoeur-Martineau, Samsung SAIL Montreal, Oct 2025). **7M params, 2 layers**: 44.6–45% ARC-AGI-1, 7.8–8% ARC-AGI-2, 85.3% Maze-Hard vs HRM's 74.5% at 27M. Won ARC Prize 2025 1st place paper award ([results](https://arcprize.org/blog/arc-prize-2025-results-analysis)); follow-up **Test-time Adaptation of TRM** arXiv [2511.02886](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.02886).
- Tofy relevance: **directly supports Tofy's core premise** — tiny networks doing many latent refinement iterations are the best-known parameter-efficiency point for ARC-style grid reasoning, and the ARC Prize's own reading is that HRM/TRM work as "latent search systems" (time-extended latent exploration). But these are static, supervised, per-task-augmented models — no environment interaction, no action conditioning.

**ARC-AGI-3 (interactive) agents:**
- **ARC-AGI-3 Technical Report** — arXiv [2603.24621](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24621v1) / [PDF at arcprize.org](https://arcprize.org/media/ARC_AGI_3_Technical_Report.pdf) (launched Mar 2026). 2025 preview competition (Jul 18–Aug 19, 2025): humans 100%; frontier LLM agents 0–0.37%; best purpose-built agent **StochasticGoose (Tufa Labs), 12.58%** — a 4-layer CNN + RL predicting *which actions change the frame* on 64×64 inputs, i.e. a crude learned action-effect model driving informed exploration. Second: **Blind Squirrel, 6.71%** — built explicit **directed state graphs from observed frames** and searched them symbolically. Report's takeaway: winners were systematic explorers/searchers, not reasoners.
- **Explore Before You Solve (AERA)** — arXiv [2605.25931](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.25931) (May 2026): three-phase EXPLORE/VERIFY/PLAN agent with a 0.5B LLM, 0.21 RHAE public / 0.30 private; also claims all 25 public games fall to "non-intelligent" strategies.
- **NVIDIA AVO** ([blog, Aug 21 2026](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-avo-reaches-100-on-arc-agi-3-demonstrating-a-frontier-level-general-purpose-architecture-for-long-horizon-autonomous-agents/)): 100 RHAE on the **public** set (25 envs, 183 levels, 6,624 actions) — but it is an **LLM harness around Claude Opus 5** with memory + supervisor loops, not a world model; NVIDIA itself cautions against reading it as a model-capability result.
- Tofy implication: **no published ARC-AGI-3 agent yet plans in a learned latent model.** The frontier on this exact benchmark is (a) brute LLM harnesses and (b) frame-change-prediction CNNs / explicit state graphs. A JEPA model + latent search would be genuinely novel there; the Blind Squirrel result also shows that on deterministic ARC-AGI-3 games, an **explicit graph over observed states is a strong, cheap baseline that any latent method must beat**.

## 4. Latent vs symbolic search head-to-head on puzzles

**DeepCubeAI (Learning Discrete World Models for Heuristic Search)** — Agostinelli & Soltani, **RLC 2024**, RL Journal vol. 4 pp. 1781–1792 ([code](https://github.com/misaghsoltani/DeepCubeAI), [poster](https://cse.sc.edu/~foresta/assets/files/Poster--LearningDiscreteWorldModelsforHeuristicSearch.pdf)); packaged in **DeepXube**, arXiv [2603.23873](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.23873).
- Domain: Rubik's Cube, **Sokoban**, IceSlider, DigitJump — from pixels.
- Planning type: **explicit search in a learned discrete latent space** — a discretized (binary) latent world model whose exactness enables state re-identification, plus a learned heuristic, driven by Q*/batch-weighted A*.
- Evidence: **100% solve rate on Sokoban, IceSlider, DigitJump and Rubik's Cube** (99.9% reversed-goal), generalizing over start/goal pairs. This is the cleanest published demonstration that classical search run *inside* a learned model matches ground-truth-model search on puzzle domains — the key trick being *discrete* latents so revisited states hash identically and model error doesn't compound.
- Tofy relevance: **the single most actionable result for Tofy's topic question.** It says latent search works on Sokoban-class puzzles *if* the latent is discrete/exact enough for re-identification — a JEPA with continuous embeddings will struggle with exactly that (state aliasing, drift over rollouts). It needed known goal states, though; Tofy's goals are hidden.
- Same lineage context: DeepCubeA (Agostinelli 2019, Nature MI) — learned heuristic + explicit symbolic search, 100% cube solves, 60.3% shortest-path; **PHS (Policy-guided Heuristic Search)** — arXiv [2103.11505](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.11505), AAAI 2021, and **Levin Tree Search with Context Models** — arXiv [2305.16945](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.16945), IJCAI 2023 (DeepMind): learned policy/heuristic + explicit search with search-effort guarantees, beating A*/W-A*/PUCT on Sokoban/Witness/sliding-tile. Post-2023 activity in this line is mostly tooling (DeepXube) and theory (learning admissible heuristics, arXiv [2509.22626](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.22626)).
- Head-to-head verdict from the literature: on deterministic puzzles, the winning recipe is consistently **learned components (model/heuristic/policy) + explicit search algorithm**, not learned search replacing explicit search. Dualformer is the lone exception (learned search cheaper than its A* teacher), and it still needed the teacher.

**PLDM (Learning from Reward-Free Offline Data: A Case for Planning with Latent Dynamics Models)** — arXiv [2502.14819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14819) (Sobal, LeCun et al., Feb 2025).
- 2D maze navigation; **explicit planning (MPC-style optimization) in a JEPA latent space** trained reconstruction-free from reward-free offline data.
- Evidence: beats model-free baselines on generalization to new layouts, suboptimal-data regimes, and 4× larger maps; follow-up analysis "What Drives Success in Physical Planning with JEPA World Models?" arXiv [2512.24497](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.24497v3).
- Tofy relevance: the closest published relative of Tofy's exact architecture (JEPA + latent planning, no rewards). But: navigation with continuous-ish dynamics, not combinatorial object-manipulation puzzles — gradient/CEM planning in continuous latent space has no published win on anything Sokoban-hard. The gap between PLDM (navigation) and DeepCubeAI (needs discrete latents for puzzles) brackets exactly where Tofy sits.

## 5. Emergent/implicit planning in small networks (interpretability, 2024–26)

- Covered above: arXiv [2407.15421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.15421), [2506.10138](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.10138), [2504.01871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01871) (Sokoban DRC).
- **Evidence of Learned Look-Ahead in a Chess-Playing Neural Network** — arXiv [2406.00877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00877) (Jenner et al., NeurIPS 2024): Leela's policy net causally represents optimal moves 2 plies ahead (92% probe accuracy on forced lines) — look-ahead in weights, no search.
- **When Can Model-Free RL be Enough for Thinking?** — arXiv [2506.17124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17124) (Hanna & Corrado, 2025): theory ("thought MDPs") for when thinking emerges without a model; policy initialization is the deciding factor; thought steps ≈ implicit policy-improvement steps.
- Consistent picture: implicit planning emerges in tiny recurrent nets on deterministic grids, scales with test-time compute, but only under dense reward and huge experience; it degrades gracefully rather than solving hard instances outright (DRC-style nets still fail hard Boxoban levels that explicit search handles).

## Conclusions for Tofy

1. **Does latent search beat pixel/symbolic search on grid puzzles?** The literature's answer is nuanced: search *in a learned model* decisively beats no-search (Thinker: 94.5% vs 56.7%), and search in a learned **discrete** latent matches ground-truth search (DeepCubeAI: 100% Sokoban). But nobody has shown *continuous* latent (JEPA-style) search winning on a Sokoban-class combinatorial puzzle; PLDM's wins are navigation-only. The evidence points to a concrete requirement for Tofy: **discreteness/re-identifiability of latent states** (hashing revisited states) is what made puzzle-latent-search work, and it composes well with cheap explicit search (A*/Q*, Levin TS) rather than learned search procedures.
2. **The strongest published ARC-AGI-3 baselines are embarrassingly simple** — frame-change CNN + exploration (12.58%) and explicit observed-state graphs (6.71%); no learned-latent planner exists on this benchmark yet. Tofy's JEPA planner has a clear novelty slot, but must beat the explicit-state-graph baseline, which on deterministic 64×64/16-color games is nearly free to build.
3. **Tiny models are not the bottleneck.** TRM (7M, 2 layers) and DRC (~1M ConvLSTM) both demonstrate grid reasoning/planning at or below Tofy's scale; ARC Prize's own analysis frames their success as *latent iterative search* — iteration depth and test-time compute matter far more than parameters or architectural hierarchy. Tofy's 1M budget is consistent with published successes provided it spends compute on iteration/search at decision time.
4. **Hidden goals are Tofy's real gap.** Every latent-search success above (Thinker, DeepCubeAI, PLDM, PHS) had either dense reward or an explicit goal state to search toward. The ARC-AGI-3 preview winners substituted "does this action change the frame" as a proxy objective. No published work solves "search a learned latent model toward a goal you must first infer" — that's the open problem, not the latent search machinery itself.
